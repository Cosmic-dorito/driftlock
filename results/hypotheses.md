# Hypothesis verification (rule R3)

Generated by `scripts/verify_hypotheses.py` against 40 pairs from the sponsor's published generator.

The facts about the sponsor's generator were derived by reading its source code. Reading is not evidence; this is.

## H1 - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** Reference footprint in the search image is exactly 100x100 px

**Evidence.** gt_box sizes observed: [(100.0, 100.0)]; reference (1000, 1000), search (1000, 1000). Ratio 1000/100 = 10, consistent with 1 nm/px vs 10 nm/px.

## H2 - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** gt = (x0/10 + 50, y0/10 + 50), x0/y0 integer; GT on a 0.1 px grid

**Evidence.** centre == box_origin + 50 for all 40 pairs. Max deviation of (origin x 10) from an integer: 0.00e+00. GT is therefore quantised to 0.1 px - sub-pixel accuracy below that cannot be demonstrated on THIS generator, which is why ours uses fractional crop origins.

## H3 - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** Noise is Poisson (shot) then Gaussian (detector)

**Evidence.** Mean-variance fit over 18 flat-window intensity bins: Var = 1.747 * mean + 425.5. Signal-dependent variance (positive slope) is the Poisson signature; a purely additive Gaussian model predicts slope 0. Predicted slope for dose=200 is 255/dose = 1.275. => Correlation on raw intensity is NOT the ML estimator; variance stabilisation (A1) is justified.

## H4a - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** INTER_AREA downscale of the reference is the correct forward operator

**Evidence.** ZNCC at the TRUE location across 40 pairs: mean 0.835, min 0.580 - a strong absolute correlation for a cross-magnification match against a 10x-noisier image. A local maximum sits within 1.0 px of the truth (max 4.1 px). So area-average downsampling does bring the two images into a common domain, exactly as predicted from the same beam PSF being applied to both before the 10x decimation. Whether that peak WINS is a separate question - see H4b.

## H4b - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** Plain ZNCC argmax is defeated by periodic ambiguity

**Evidence.** 10/40 pairs mis-locate by more than 5 px (25%). Median error 1.10 px, max 271.71 px. Worst case (id=20): error 271.71 px, with the true location scoring 0.9064 against a wrong location at 0.9188 - a margin of only 0.0124 (1.3%). The correct answer was present but ranked second. => Retaining top-K candidates instead of the argmax (A6) is necessary, not decorative.

## H5 - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** The search image is noisier / more degraded than the reference

**Evidence.** dose reference=2000 vs search=200 (10x lower). Estimated noise sigma: reference 7.26, search 29.05 (4.0x). Degradation parameters in this run: {'shear_amplitude_px': '1.5', 'drift_jitter_px': '0.5', 'gamma': '1.0', 'vignette_strength': '0.0', 'barrel_distortion_k': '0.0', 'speckle_sigma': '0.0', 'salt_pepper_prob': '0.0', 'charging_streak_prob': '0.0'}. NOTE: the defaults here leave gamma/vignette/barrel/speckle/S&P/streaks at zero - they are available but OFF unless requested, so robustness to them must be tested deliberately.

## H7 - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** Aperiodic fingerprint exists: the true location is distinguishable in principle

**Evidence.** Self-correlation margin between the true alignment and the best lattice-equivalent impostor, over 12 references: median 0.0574, min 0.0086, max 0.2252. All are strictly positive, so the random-walk line placement does break exact periodicity and disambiguation is possible - but the margin is small, which is precisely why argmax fails (H4b) and why the aperiodic residual has to be scored explicitly (A7).

## H8 - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** Periodic ambiguity makes a mis-lock a hard failure, not a near miss

**Evidence.** Across 40 pairs the strongest rival peak sits 45.5 px from the winner (min 7.0), and 100% are beyond the 5 px pass threshold. The score margin between winner and rival is only 0.0162 (min 0.0002). => Competing hypotheses are separated by far more than the tolerance but by far less than the noise in the score, so picking wrong costs the whole pair. That ratio is the case for a confidence measure (A8) rather than a bare coordinate.

## H9 - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** Their generator has no rotation and no scale variation

**Evidence.** Manifest columns contain no rotation field (False) and no scale field (False). The magnification ratio is fixed at 10 by the 1 nm/px vs 10 nm/px pixel sizes. The problem statement says 9:1-11:1 and 1-2 degrees WILL be tested, so this data cannot exercise that envelope - which is exactly why we build our own generator.

## H10 - CONFIRMED

**Claim.** Raster shear produces a systematic, correctable sub-pixel x bias

**Evidence.** Over 30 correctly-located pairs: dx mean -0.837 (sd 0.477) versus dy mean +0.073 (sd 0.224) - the x error is biased while y is not. Regressing dx on gt_y gives slope -0.00165 px/px with Pearson r = -0.861; the shear model predicts -0.00150 px/px for shear_amplitude_px=1.5. Sign and magnitude agree. => Most of the baseline's ~1 px median error is a CORRECTABLE systematic bias rather than noise, and because the distortion is a shear, the refinement must be affine (cv2.MOTION_AFFINE), not Euclidean. Caveat: n=30, so the slope estimate carries wide error bars; the correlation, not the exact slope, is the finding.
